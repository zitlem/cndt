# 0020: A vise per setup, checked in the simulation

- **Status:** Accepted (the owner said "merge, continue" on 2026-11-18; `docs/safety.md` listed fixture collisions as not yet checked)
- **Date:** 2026-11-18

## Context

Simulation checks the tool, shank and holder against the stock and the part. Nothing checks them against what holds the part. On a first cut the most likely crash after a wrong offset is a contour or a drill run into a vise jaw, because the jaws stand up the part's sides exactly where outside profiles go.

## Decision

A setup may hold a **vise**: `{"grip", "along", "jaw"}`, in the setup's program coordinates.

- **`grip`**: how much of the stock the jaws hold, in millimetres from the stock's bottom up. The jaws' top is the stock's bottom plus the grip.
- **`along`**: `x` (the default, the jaws clamp the stock's front and back faces) or `y` (its left and right).
- **`jaw`**: each jaw's thickness away from the stock, 25 mm by default. The jaws run the whole length of the simulated area.

The jaws are placed on the real stock (for a side setup, its exact box in that setup's coordinates, not the larger planning box). The simulation grid reaches past the stock by the thickest jaw on every side, the same for every setup, so carried stock still maps cell onto cell. The cells in that margin hold no stock.

The simulation then checks every sampled tool position against the jaws: the cutter's profile within its radius, the end of the flutes out to the shank, and the holder's face out to the holder. Any part of the tool coming down onto a jaw is a **fixture** collision, a **problem** that blocks posting without an override. The jaws are never cut. One finding is kept per operation, its deepest.

The command `cam.set_vise {setup, grip?, along?, jaw?}` sets or removes it (no grip removes it), and each setup card in the app has a vise field. File version 21.

## Options considered

- **Fixture as general solids (STEP of the vise, clamps).** Exact and general, but needs a fixture library, placing and a way to pick it. A later step.
- **A parametric vise per setup** (chosen). It covers most first parts on a mill, is simple to enter (one number), and works through the same height map as the stock.

## Consequences

- **Parallels** (added 2026-11-18): the stock is taken to sit on two parallels under its clamped faces, 4 mm thick each, standing against the jaws, their tops at the stock's bottom. A tool going below the stock's bottom over them (a through hole near the clamped edge, a contour too deep) hits them, a problem like a jaw. The gap between them is open. 4 mm is a typical parallel's thickness; it is fixed, not entered, and the setup sheet states it.
- **Body and floor** (added 2026-11-19, file version 31): the vise may have a `body`, one number: how far below the stock's bottom the top of its fixed body stands, in millimetres (`cam.set_vise {…, body}`; none by default, above 0 and at most 500). The body fills the gap between the parallels along the whole simulated area, so any part of the tool going below that depth is a **fixture** problem, like a jaw. Why agent-accepted: it extends an Accepted record on the same height map, adds no dependency, and only adds a check. A vise without `body` behaves as before and its file is byte-identical; a version 30 build refuses a file with a body and says to update (see decision 0009, version 31).
- Not yet: clamps and fixture plates, and any fixture on the B table itself.
- The setup sheet can show the vise in a later change.
